SURVIVAL CHAOS  /  BOSS ENCOUNTER  /  T+600s  /  AS BUILT

LEVIATHAN

The model already had three separate weapon banks, and they happened to sit at the floor, the middle and the ceiling of the playable band. That was the entire fight, waiting to be wired as one. It is wired now — and this is what it does.

The arena, measured

Three facts decide everything else

You cannot outrun a bullet

Boss projectiles orbit at 80°/s; the player at 29.2°/s. A shot laps the world in 4.5 s, the player in 12.3 s. Everything the boss fires comes back around and hits you in the back. Resogun calls this the Cylinder Wrap and treats it as a design rule. Here it was already true by accident, and the cadences below are set against it.

A shot never changes lane

Projectiles orbit with RotateAround, which preserves the distance from the axis they were born at — exactly, and for their whole life. The player is pinned to 137.200, and with both hitboxes a shot can only touch them if it was born between 133.9 and 140.5. A shot fired off that lane misses forever. This is the fact that had been quietly costing the boss three quarters of its fire.

You cannot fly over it

The hull trigger is 153 units tall against a playable band of 89. It spans y 12–165; you live in y 44–133. The boss is a moving wall — the Stormruler shape, already built. The only way past it is around, which makes the direction flip the most important key on the board.

The rig

Three banks, one third of the band each

The 32 enemyShootPivot transforms are not scattered. They cluster into three groups at three heights, and each group lands almost exactly on a third of the band the player can occupy. Nothing about the current fight knows this.

Before

It was a wall that sprays

All three banks fired every muzzle simultaneously, forever, on fixed 0.5 s and 3 s timers. No phases, no telegraph, no weak points, no reason to be at any particular height. The hull took damage anywhere, so the correct play was to hold the trigger and hope, and 300 HP was the only thing making it last. Everything below is the same 32 muzzles, fired differently.

As built

Armoured → Exposed → Scuttle

Three acts out of one 300-point pool, so the bar moves throughout and retuning an emplacement cannot quietly change what the boss is worth. The Resogun rule underneath is the one all five of its bosses share: the hull is not the target — specific, colour-coded things on it are, and destroying one takes an attack out of the fight. BossPhaseState holds the order with no Unity object attached, because “once, and never backwards” is the kind of rule that is impossible to check by playing.

1

ARMOURED — 150 HP across three weak points

The hull is invulnerable; bullets spark off it and die. Three neon-green weak points sit at the centroid of each bank, 50 HP each, and they are the only things that take damage. Killing one silences that bank permanently and the arena gets measurably quieter.

The tension is geometric and comes free from the rig: player bullets travel at the player’s own height, so to kill a bank you have to fly at that bank’s height — which is precisely where its own fire is thickest. Each attack below is densest in its own third of the band.

Each pod sits at the centroid of its own bank’s muzzle heights, averaged from the model at build time rather than typed in, so it cannot drift away from the guns it stands for — world y 53.8, 91.7 and 127.1. They are mounted proud of the hull, which is not decoration: the hull is a trigger that eats bullets, so a target inside it could never be hit at all.

KeelCurtain 12 muzzles · one row open · cycle 6.0 s

All twelve keel muzzles except one row, so the wall has exactly one gap in it. The open row steps up one each volley and wraps, which makes it learnable rather than random. The rows are read off the muzzle heights rather than authored, because the second copy of a fact is the one that goes stale. Because a shot wraps in 4.5 s and the cycle is 6 s, the previous curtain comes back round just before the next one leaves — the player is threaded between two walls for most of the phase. This is the Cylinder Wrap turned into a mechanic.

CrownRake 16 muzzles · 0.12 s per row · cycle 4.0 s

The same 16 muzzles, fired row by row instead of all at once, with the direction alternating each volley. A slab becomes a climbing staircase you dive under, or a descending one you climb over. This is the real fix for a volley reading as one solid mass — not a size change, a timing change.

ProwLance 4 muzzles · charge 1.2 s · stream 0.4 s · cycle 5.0 s

The laser, timed rather than triggered. The prow pod swells for the whole 1.2 s wind-up — that swell is the telegraph, and it is the only one, because a muzzle is an empty transform with nothing to light up. Then a dense stream fires. The design said the lance captured your height during the charge; it does not. The boss holds altitude now, so what the lance actually does is own the middle of the band, which nothing else in the fight reaches. The 1.2 s telegraph is one full band crossing at 70 u/s: always escapable, never for free. Shoot the pod off mid-charge and the warning stops with it, rather than leaving the player dodging a shot that is never coming.

2

EXPOSED — 120 HP on the core

All three weak points down. Every gun goes quiet, the boss holds a 2 s silence — every attack restarts its cadence from there, which is the beat the player is given to notice the fight changed shape — and the hull starts taking damage. This is Rotatron’s “it opens up” beat and Stormruler’s shed-armour beat in one moment. Anything mid-volley is stopped rather than allowed to finish: a lance still streaming out of an emplacement that was destroyed to end the phase is the exact thing the phase change is supposed to have stopped.

HullRam telegraph 1.0 s · charge 3.0 s · cycle 7.0 s

The hull flashes — by this act there are no emplacements left to light up — then it triples its orbit speed to 45°/s and sweeps around the ring at you. That is faster than the player’s 29.2°/s, so running the same way loses. The hull spans the whole band so climbing loses too. The counter is to dash straight through it — Doomsphere’s bull-fight, played as Resogun plays it. The dash is built: 0.22 s of invincibility covering 74.5 world units against a 70.5-unit hull, which is the measurement it was sized to.

ArenaWreckage a plate every 1.8 s · each lasts 16 s

Armoured is a fight about height; Exposed threw that away, because the hull spans the whole band and any altitude is as good as another. Wreckage puts the three heights back with their meaning reversed: the boss sheds plates of its own hull from the emplacements the player themselves destroyed, at those emplacements’ own heights. The places worth flying to become the places worth avoiding, and the player already knows where they are.

They do not drift. Everything else here rides the ring; these are the one thing that does not, which is what makes them read as debris rather than as another attacker — and it means the boss, cruising at 15°/s while it sheds, leaves a wake of its own hull and eventually comes back round into it. Life over cadence is the tuning: 16 s against 1.8 holds about nine plates, three at any one height, which against a 12.3 s lap is a forced move every four seconds or so. Three shots break one, and breaking it earns nothing — clearing a lane is the whole of its reward. Which pod sheds next counts over the wrecked ones rather than indexing all three and skipping, so the order the player killed them in cannot change how much wreckage they face.

3

SCUTTLE — last 30 HP

Every magazine on the ship cooks off at once: all 32 muzzles fire simultaneous volleys on a 1.2 s cycle regardless of which emplacements are wrecked, until it dies. Loud, short, and survivable if you keep moving. It exists so the kill has a crescendo instead of a health bar quietly reaching zero. Plates already in the air ride into it on their own clocks, so the last act starts inside the mess the second one made.

Built & verified

The dash

Space, or the right shoulder / south button on a pad. A committed burst along the heading captured at the moment it starts, invincible for its whole length.

Why it lives on the movement, not the ship

The ship and the Main Camera run the same PlayerMovement component at the same authored speed, and that identity is the whole of how the camera keeps station. A dash applied to the ship alone flings it out of frame five times faster than the camera can follow — the same bug as an unshared speed pick, arriving in a fifth of a second instead of over a run. So the burst is shared static state, and the measured drift is zero rather than small.

The heading is captured, not read

Taken once, when the burst begins, and normalised. Letting go halfway through does not strand the ship and grabbing a new direction halfway through does not steer it. It also gives the burst a direction on the frames where nothing is held, where it falls back to the way the nose is pointing — so a dash always goes somewhere.

Player side

Ways to defeat it

Choose your remaining fight

Three weak points means three opening moves, and the order is the difficulty dial the player sets themselves. Kill the Prow first and you remove the only attack that punishes standing still — the safe opening. Kill the Crown first and you own the top of the band for the rest of the phase. Kill the Keel first and the curtains stop, but you spend the whole fight under the rake.

A verb that arrives late

Everything the ship could do before the dash was continuous — orbit, climb, flip — so every threat was answered by having started moving earlier, and none could be answered now. That is why nothing in the fight was worth telegraphing. The dash is the answer that arrives late, and it is what makes a 1.2 s Lance charge a decision rather than a countdown.

Upgrades read on the boss

A pod is 7 units across, so the sextuple spread lands maybe four of six on a centred volley and none on one aimed a body-length off. At stage 0 and 0.5 s between volleys that is a long minute per emplacement; fully upgraded it is a handful of seconds. The boss is where twenty picks of Attack Speed and Shot Upgrade finally show up as something other than a bigger number.

The bar stays honest

300 total: 50 apiece on the three emplacements, then 120 on the open hull, then the last 30 to the Scuttle. One pool, so the bar moves throughout Armoured rather than sitting full while the player does real work — and so retuning a pod cannot quietly change what the boss is worth.

Found while measuring

Five things the build turned up

All five were invisible from the outside. Four are fixed; the fifth is a correction to this page.

The guns did not turn with the ship — fixed

EnemySpaceShip rotates the NAVEBOSS model ±90° to face its direction of travel, but all 32 pivots hung off the Boss root, which only ever faces the middle of the arena. So when RingChase reversed the boss, the ship turned and the guns stayed put — the prow muzzle swinging 87 world units. A telegraphed lance is meaningless if the muzzle is not where the barrel is, so this blocked everything else. The muzzles hang off a rig carrying the same EnemySpaceShip component now — not a second copy of the rule kept in step with the first, the same rule read once in the same frame. Every muzzle’s position in the boss’s own frame is identical to three decimals; mirroring is all it adds.

Three quarters of the boss’s fire could not reach the player — fixed

A shot keeps the radius it was born at, forever. The muzzles are spread across a hull 30 units deep and sit from 131.6 to 150.2, against a hittable lane of 133.9–140.5. Only 8 of the 32 were inside it — keel 3 of 12, prow 1 of 4, crown 4 of 16 — so a curtain of twelve arrived as a wall of three, the rake had four of its sixteen steps, and the lance fired one muzzle in four. The fight’s own vocabulary was describing something the player never faced.

The muzzles do not move, because that is the one thing the rig must not do. The shot leaves the barrel and then eases onto the lane, radius only, through the same easing every ship already uses on its way to the ring. It is off unless asked for, so the player’s own rounds are untouched by construction. Measured after: 87–100% of boss fire in the lane, against about a quarter before.

The emplacements swapped lanes with the heading — fixed

A pod is mounted proud of the hull along the tangent, and the arena is a circle, so 39.5 units out from a point on a 137-unit ring leaves you 5.6 units outside it. The builder takes that back off — but the correction was authored on the mirroring rig, so it mirrored too, and in one heading it was added outward instead of subtracted inward. That does not cancel the error, it doubles it: 137.3 travelling one way, 148.0 the other. It is re-applied every frame against the direction the middle of the arena is actually in now. The mirroring that was wanted survives — the keel pod sits at +3.95 and −3.95 along the ring, and at +0.56 inward in both.

Wreckage billed a collision many times over — fixed

The first guess was the plate’s shape, on the theory that a tumbling box sweeps the player in and out of itself. The collider became a sphere and it changed nothing. What turns is the player: the ship is aimed at the arena’s axis every frame and rolled by SpaceShipPitch, so a ship sitting inside a stationary trigger keeps carrying its own collider back out through the surface and in again, and every one of those is a fresh OnTriggerEnter. With the arena verifiably empty, one plate took a stationary player from 19 hit points to 0 in 0.64 s. A plate goes intangible while the player is inside it now; the same test costs exactly one hit.

Two silent races in the emitter — fixed

BossEmitter resolved the health bar once, in Start, and Timer switches that bar on at the same second the spawn stream releases the boss. A tag search does not see inactive objects, so whichever ran first decided whether the boss had a working bar for the whole fight — and the failure is silent, because the fight plays correctly and the bar just sits full. And HitFlash claimed every renderer beneath it, which would have meant a shot breaking on the armour lighting the three pods as brightly as a shot that hit one. That distinction is the whole of the first act.

The orphaned trigger volume — deleted

27b8a93 added a “Laser Trigger” sphere to the prefab; 2d44065 deleted ColliderScript_3, the only thing that read it. The GameObject sat on the prefab carrying a broken MonoBehaviour reference. It is gone.

Scope

What is deliberately not taken from Resogun

  • Voxel shatter. The hull is a single four-material mesh, not voxels. Destroyed emplacements go dark and drop wreckage rather than exploding into cubes.
  • Rotatron’s interior phase. The model is solid and the arena is a ring the boss sits on, not a cylinder it forms. There is nothing to fly inside.
  • Instant-kill hitboxes. This game has a health bar and twenty levels of upgrades built around surviving hits. Contact costs one hit point, as it does now.
  • Serpentinus. A segmented body that slithers round the ring is a second boss, not a phase of this one.
Still open

What has not been settled

The plates read only as a silhouette

They carry the boss’s own hull material, which is nearly black. Against the sky or the lava they are unmistakable; against the dark island one can be lost. An emissive tint would fix it, and would also make them the first thing in the arena that glows because it is dangerous rather than because it is hot — a call about the art direction rather than about the mechanic, so it has deliberately not been made.

Measured, not played

Every figure here comes from a probe with the player parked and the arena controlled — nine plates, a forced move every four seconds, 36.5 s of standing still at the keel’s own height under the new lane. Standing still is the worst case and nobody plays that way. The shed cadence, the lane response and a plate’s life are one serialized field each for exactly that reason.

No charge sound

The lance and the ram both telegraph visually and neither makes a noise. Adding one means a new SoundDefinition and a curated clip, and picking that by ear is not a decision to take on somebody’s behalf.

What the guns cost, still unmeasured

Roughly four times as much boss fire now reaches the player as did before. The damage rate against a stationary target is known; what a competent player actually loses across the three acts is not, and that is the number that decides whether any of this is tuned right.
